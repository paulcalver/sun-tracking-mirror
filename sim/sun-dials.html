Each dial is one GM2804 with its own field-oriented-control driver, a 12 V supply and no gears. The encoder gives the disc angle straight away at power-up, so no homing sensor is needed. Sizes are from retailer listings (about 35 mm across, 12 mm for the motor, about 25 mm with encoder); how the encoder mounts and which part is the fixed shaft are assumed, so check them on the real motor.

Place & display

Drive: each disc is turned directly by a GM2804 gimbal motor, so the disc itself turns and the black line is its hand. The counts above are what a 14-bit encoder would read (0.022 degrees per count). Pure elevation: the hand sits on the right and its angle from the horizontal is the elevation, so it swings up and back down each day. Elevation (flips) mode: the hand points at the sun's height above the horizon, on the left in the morning (east) and the right in the afternoon (west); it points down below the horizon (the line stays black). Sky view: the hand points at the sun's position projected on a south-facing wall, so it passes straight over the top without flipping. Azimuth is the compass bearing from the observer (north up, east right). All positions are as seen from Earth at the chosen place. Positions use low-precision solar and lunar formulas (about 0.1° sun, 0.3° moon, a few arcminutes for planets).
