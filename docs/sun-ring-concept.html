Sun ring on a wall arm: a ring with a dot for the Sun

The ring (or, in wire mode, just the Sun on wires) is carried out from the wall on an arm. The arm tip stands for you. The Sun orbits through the day and shifts along the pole through the year. Drag to orbit the view.

Bearing: 0 = wall faces north, 90 = east, 180 = south, 270 = west. The compass letters stay fixed; the wall turns. The green disc is the horizon through the ring centre, the dark half of the ring is below it (night). Wire mode: the Sun hangs from wires that run to anchors on an overhead frame; the orbit is only implied (dotted). Wires turn red when a tension would go negative, meaning the Sun would swing instead of hanging there. Anchors on the wall alone cannot work: gravity would swing the Sun back against the wall. Tensions are in units of the Sun's weight; with 4 wires the tensions are not unique, so the page shows the spread that keeps the slackest wire as tight as possible. Pointer mode: a stiff rod hinged at the pole tip (a two-axis gimbal in the bead) points at the Sun. It spins once a day about the pole axis and tilts by the declination through the year, so its tip is on the exact daily circle. The tip never touches anything; the page reports the clearance to the wall and the drop below the pole, and the sag of a 3 mm carbon rod against a 3 mm light fibre. Ring models: "slides along pole" keeps the ring in the equator plane and moves it along the pole axis by tan(declination) each day, so from the joint the ring dot is exactly on the real Sun. "Leans" is the great-circle ring, which only matches at the equinoxes. With "Camera follows wall" on you always look at the wall head-on, as a viewer would.
