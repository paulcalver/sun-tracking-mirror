# Wall piece: 3 rows of eight 50 mm mirrors, one NEMA 17 per row (2026-11-17)

Draft, nothing built or tested. CAD: `cad/src/spinning_mirror_wall_row_50.py`; STEP and part files in `cad/out/spinning-mirror/spinning-mirror-wall-row-50/`; previews in `cad/media/previews/spinning-mirror-wall-row-50/`; BOM `bom/bom-spinning-mirror-wall-row-50.csv` (one line per screw type, quantities for all 24 units); first-test shopping list and prices in `spinning-mirror-belt-row-test-bom.md` (priced for a 10-unit row; scale it to the BOM). Background: `spinning-mirror-150-nema17.md` (the single unit and motor).

## Layout
- Three **2020 aluminium extrusion rails** (680 mm each) run along the wall, 200 mm apart vertically, the spindle line 45 mm out from the wall. The rail is the structure and the slide. **Three vertical 2020 uprights** (440 mm, at X = 40, 360 and 600 mm, in the gaps between units and one past the last unit) stand 5 mm off the wall behind the rails and join the three rows into one rigid frame. The ring sweep stays at least 6 mm clear of them (checked at random ring angles). Each rail ties back to each upright through a small printed **rail tie** that sits on the rail in the gap between units: two M5 x 12 screws down into T-nuts in the rail's top slot and one M5 x 35 along Y into a T-nut in the upright's front slot. Two printed **wall plates** per upright (one M5 x 12 into the upright's back slot, two M4 wall screws) fix it to the wall. The motor end has no upright; the rails overhang the first upright by about 100 mm.
- Eight units per row at **80 mm pitch**, 24 in all. Each is a printed bearing block that bolts into the rail's top T-slot with two M5 slide-in T-nuts, so it slides to set the belt tension. A 5 mm spindle runs in two 625ZZ bearings in the block.
- **Drive:** each spindle carries a double-head GT2 20-tooth pulley. Eight short closed 200 mm GT2 loops chain a row: the NEMA 17 turns the first unit through a single pulley (lower belt level), unit 1 turns unit 2 (upper level), and so on, alternating. Every spindle in the row turns the same way. The belts and pulleys are exposed.
- Each row has its own **NEMA 17** hanging below a printed bracket at the left end of the rail, shaft up, 80 mm before unit 1. The bracket's 6 mm web is the motor plate, same screws and counterbores as the 150 mm pedestal. Three steppers, three TMC2209 drivers, one ESP32: the rows can run the same pattern or different ones.
- **Ring and mirror:** a ring (66 mm swept diameter) clamped to the top of the spindle by one M3 grub screw into a heat-set insert, with a free 3 mm axle on two 683ZZ bearings carrying a 4 mm printed carrier and a 50 mm x 1 mm glass mirror on each face. No balance trim screws and no brass slug at this size; weigh the discs.
- **Ring angle:** every ring is fitted at the same angle (ring plane parallel to the rail, use a straight edge along the rail when you tighten the grub screws), so only the tumble differs.
- Dimensions: each row is about 0.76 m wide (rail from X = -56 to 624, motor to X = -106); rings are 66 mm across with 14 mm between them and 12 mm to the wall at the nearest; ring centre 117.5 mm above its rail bottom, ring top 150.5, so there is about 50 mm between a row's ring tops and the next rail up. The whole piece is about 0.75 m tall including the top row. Change `ROWS`, `N` and `ROW_PITCH` at the top of the script to resize.

## Electronics box
A printed box (124 x 78 x 44 mm outside, 118 x 72 x 35 mm inside) hangs under the bottom rail beside the motor end, flush against the rail bottom, held by two M5 x 12 screws into slide-in T-nuts in the rail's bottom slot (heads recessed in the 6 mm top plate). It clears the motor and the wall plates; the top of the box just touches the bottom ends of the uprights. The lid is the base: it carries four posts with heat-set inserts for a 70 x 90 mm perfboard (82 x 62 mm holes) holding the ESP32 and three TMC2209 drivers on sockets, and it has vent slots; four M3 x 8 screws go into corner posts. The right-hand end wall has a 12 mm hole for the DC jack (the Pi Hut jack-to-screw-terminal adapter has no panel flange, so it is glued or clipped behind the hole) and an 11 x 4.6 mm slot for USB-C; the three motor leads leave through three 14 mm holes in the left wall. The motors of rows 2 and 3 need 4-pin extension cables (about 1 m) run up the wall. The ESP32 and driver placeholders are only for fit. Change the board size, hole spacing or connector positions in the parameter block at the top of the script.

## Checks the script runs (`--check`)
No clashes at any of three random tumble poses (and two more with random ring angles) of all 24 units (axle and bearings excepted by design), none in the static pose, and every printed part is a single solid. Mass about 3.5 kg for rails, uprights, brackets, motors, belts and box plus 2.2 kg for the 24 units, so about 5.7 kg on the wall.

## Load
One unit turns about 50 g (mirror group 17 g) with spin inertia about 1.1e-5 to 1.3e-5 kg m2. With the rotor a row is about 1.1e-4 kg m2, so 100 rad/s2 needs 0.011 N m and 300 rad/s2 needs 0.034 N m, a small part of the 0.25 N m a motor should give on 24 V. Friction, not inertia, decides how far a row scales: bearings, belt tension and tooth losses over eight links. Measure it (see the 150 mm test plan). Three motors at 1.2 to 1.5 A need the 24 V 5 A supply in the BOM.

## Printing (PETG or ASA; PLA is fine for the first test)
- **Ring:** flat, +Y face up, 4 walls; ream the two 7.05 mm bearing pockets and the 5.1 mm spindle bore. Heat-set insert in the foot. Print 24.
- **Carrier:** flat. **Spindle block:** upright; the lower bearing pocket has a 45 degree ceiling so it needs no bridge or support; ream the pockets so a 625ZZ presses in. Print 24 of each.
- **Motor bracket** (3): upside down on its flat top face. **Rail tie** (9): as modelled. **Wall plate** (6): flat on the bed. **Electronics box:** upside down on the flat top plate; **lid:** flat.
- The files in `parts/print_ready/` are already in these orientations and carry their part names. Y-up versions for Fusion are in `parts/print/`.

## Assembly and tension
1. The T-nuts are slide-in, so load them first from the open ends. Each upright: three nuts in the front slot (one per rail tie) and two in the back slot (one per wall plate). Each rail's top slot (from the right-hand end): the bracket pair, two per tie, then two per unit; the bottom slot of the lowest rail takes the two box nuts. Build the frame on the bench: bolt the wall plates to the uprights, lay the three rails on the uprights' ties (200 mm apart, rail bottoms at 0, 200 and 400 mm up the upright), and tighten the ties. Then hang the whole frame on the wall (the lowest rail bottom is the datum), fit each motor to its bracket and bolt the brackets to the rail ends.
2. Fit the 625ZZ pair, the spindle, two 1 mm M5 washers as a 2 mm spacer on the upper bearing, then the pulley (grub screw into a flat on the rod). Fit units one at a time from the motor end.
3. Set each belt before the next unit: slide the new block until the loop is firm (about 10 to 20 N, a light twang), then tighten its two M5 screws. One block's position sets both the belt to its left and the belt to its right.
4. Fit the ring with the same angle on every unit, then the mirrors.

## Open points
- The double-head pulley is assumed 28 mm long (two 8 mm bands, a 10 mm boss, 1 mm flanges), 12 mm over the teeth and 16 mm over the flanges. Check the delivered part and the belt heights (49 and 67 mm).
- Motor shaft length (24 mm assumed) and the front-face thread depth, as for the single rig.
- Belt pull on the motor shaft (rated 28 N sideways): the first pulley sits close to the motor face, but keep an eye on the first loop's tension.
- 625ZZ friction over eight units, belt tooth fit with 200 mm loops at 80 mm centres (tension range is a few mm either way), and whether the 4 mm printed carrier and ring wobble at speed.
- 24 glass mirrors turning at head height is a breakage risk: wear eye protection and consider a guard where people can reach.
- Wall fixing (plugs, studs): about 5.5 kg on six wall plates (twelve M4 screws); check the wall type, and hit studs if you can. The rail height and the row spacing are yours to choose.
