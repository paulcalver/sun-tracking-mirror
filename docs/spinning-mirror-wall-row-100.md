# 100 mm wall piece: six mirrors on one rail, a NEMA 17 at each end (six is the main build from 2026-11-24; eight is the alternative)

Draft, nothing built or tested. The 100 mm version of the wall piece in `spinning-mirror-wall-row-50.md`; read that for the drive, tension, printing and assembly details, which carry over. Build it with `python3 cad/src/spinning_mirror_wall_row_50.py --mirror100` (add `--check` for the clash sweep; the full build takes a few minutes). Output: `cad/out/spinning-mirror/spinning-mirror-wall-row-100/`, previews in `cad/media/previews/spinning-mirror-wall-row-100/`, BOM `bom/spinning-mirrors/bom-spinning-mirror-wall-row-100.csv` (`qty_needed` is the six-mirror build; `qty_needed_8_mirror` and `eight_mirror_change` show the eight-mirror alternative). The eight-mirror version (four units per motor, 1150 mm rail, about 1.38 m wide) is built with `--mirror100 --eight` into `cad/out/spinning-mirror/spinning-mirror-wall-row-100-eight/` and has no BOM of its own. **The sections below were written for the eight-mirror layout: where they give positions or counts (rail length, standoff and cable positions, unit counts, mass), the eight-mirror numbers apply; the six-mirror numbers are in the next section.**

## Electronics box: sliding tray under a lid plate (2026-11-24, six-mirror build)
The box is two parts that close to one plain **225 x 61 x 44 mm cuboid**. The **lid plate** (top, 6 mm) is screwed to the rail. The **tray** (bottom) holds everything and **slides forward off the plate** for service. The tray's interior is **209 x 55 x 35 mm**, centred under the rail (X = 237.5 to 462.5, centre X = 350), and its back is 39.5 mm from the wall.
- **Slide:** a **dovetail ridge** under the plate runs in a **dovetail groove** cut into the top of each end wall (the end walls are 8 mm thick, with a 4 mm inner rib along the top 9 mm (its underside slopes at 45 degrees, so the tray prints without support) so the wall is 12 mm thick round the groove and keeps about 3 mm of PLA each side of it; the interior is only 8 mm narrower, 201 mm, in that top band, which nothing uses), so nothing shows outside. The ridge is 2.5 mm deep and 2.4 mm wide at the plate, widening at 30 degrees from vertical (prints without support). The groove is closed 2 mm in from the **front** face, which is the stop, and open at the **back** (the wall side), so the front of the box shows no joint. Pull the tray forward, away from the wall: the ridges slide out of the open back end of the grooves.
- **Fit:** a friction fit, no catch. Each flank has 0.18 mm clearance (`DT_CL` in the script; tune after a test print, and a dab of wax or PTFE grease helps PLA on PLA). The slide is horizontal, so nothing pulls the tray out.
- **Cables:** the motor leads and the supply cable stay connected, so leave about 100 mm of slack and slide the tray out on them.
Positions are mm from the tray's inside left face (lx) and the middle of the depth (the rail centreline); everything sits on the tray floor.
- **DC socket:** a panel-mount 5.5 x 2.1 mm socket (part not chosen) in the **middle of the back wall (lx = 104.5)**, 21 mm above the floor so its body clears the breadboard; the hole is **7.8 mm** (`EB_DC_D`) through a **3 mm** wall (`EB_DC_WALL`, a thicker pad is added inside if you raise it). There is no fuse holder: the supply has its own protection.
- **Breadboard** 82 x 45 mm (half-size, one power strip removed, adhesive back), stuck flat to the tray floor at **lx 18 to 100**, middle of the depth (5 mm spare front and back). XIAO at the **left** end (lx 18 to 39) with its USB-C socket in the **left wall** (plug room 18 mm), the two TMC2209 drivers at lx 43 to 68 and 71 to 96. Parts reach 24.5 mm above the breadboard top, under the 35 mm interior.
- **Two Wago 221-415** standing on their 8.4 x 18.6 mm end (30 mm tall) in floor cradles at **lx 122 and 138**, wires leaving front and back; each has two thin walls round it (lever face and back), the wire faces are open. Clip the levers shut before pushing the blocks into the cradles.
- **Buck converter** 24 V to 5 V, 45 x 32 mm, flat, lx 150 to 195, wires out of a long side; six vent slots in the floor under it.
- **Motor leads** go up through two 13 x 7 mm slots in the lid plate at lx 48 and 84 (over the drivers), straight into the rail slot. The leads reach the driver screw terminals under them.
- **Rail screws:** two M5 through the plate at lx 64 and 144 (X = 310 and 390), heads recessed in the plate and tightened from below. With the tray off there is nothing in the way, so they are always reachable. Hang the plate on the rail first, then slide the tray on.
Clash sweep: 0 clashes and every printed part a single solid.

## Six-mirror layout (main build)
One **870 mm** 2020 rail (X = -85 to 785; buying 880 mm leaves 5 mm each end for the cut), the spindle line 70 mm from the wall. **Six units at 140 mm pitch** (X = 0 to 700), **three per motor**, motors at each end on the same brackets. Three wall standoffs at about X = 70, 350 and 630, the electronics box centred at about X = 350, and each cable run about 0.37 m, so one 1000 mm slot cover covers both runs. About 1.9 kg of frame plus 1.3 kg of units. Hinge, ring, carrier and block are identical to the eight-mirror version.

## Layout
One 1150 mm 2020 rail (X = -85 to 1065; the motor hangs about 55 mm past each rail end, supported by the 40 mm of bracket that sits on the rail) on three standoffs (X = 70, 490, 910), the spindle line 70 mm from the wall. **Eight units at 140 mm pitch.** A NEMA 17 sits at each end (X = -140 and 1120) on a bracket, the right-hand bracket being the mirror image (`print_motor_bracket_2`). Each motor drives its own **four** units through a chain of 320 mm GT2 loops (motor to unit 1 to 2 to 3 to 4, and mirrored from the right), with the middle 140 mm gap left without a belt. The electronics box (two drivers, one ESP32) hangs under the middle of the rail: power in through the back wall and USB-C on the right wall, and the two motor leads go up through the top plate into the rail slot, so each motor lead is about 0.6 m from the box (use the 0.5 m extension cables). The piece is about 0.45 m tall and 1.38 m wide.

## What changes from the 50 mm piece
- **Mirrors:** 100 mm x 1.5 mm **acrylic** by default (glass at 2 mm would add about 40 g a unit and make the torque problem worse; the thickness and material are in the 100 mm block at the top of the script).
- **Spindle:** an **8 mm rod** in two **608ZZ** (8 x 22 x 7) bearings, spaced 25 mm apart in a taller block (32 mm), with an 8 mm bore double-head pulley and an **M8 x 12 x 1 shim washer** as the spacer (12 mm OD, so it sits on the inner race only). The ring is held by an **M4** grub screw into an M4 heat-set insert, on a flat filed on the rod.
- **Hinge:** a **4 mm axle** on two **684ZZ** (4 x 9 x 4) bearings in a 12 mm wide ring with 14 mm bosses; 6 mm carrier with 5.4 mm hubs; the axle bore through the carrier is 4.15 mm with a flat-topped teardrop roof (printed holes shrink and a sideways bore sags), so the 4 mm rod slides in and is glued; ream with a 4.0 mm drill only if it is tight. The rod is **114 mm** (not the full 118 mm ring width): it ends 2 mm inside the ring's outer face, and leaves the ring through a **6.0 mm exit hole** (teardrop roof) so the turning rod cannot rub the printed wall.
- **Pitch and belts:** **140 mm** pitch (22 mm between the 118 mm swept rings) on **320 mm** closed GT2 loops with 20-tooth pulleys. Check 160-tooth 320 mm loops are sold; if not, the pitch follows the nearest loop (a 300 mm loop gives 130 mm, a 340 mm loop on 30-tooth pulleys 140 mm).
- **Block screws** are M5 x 35; the wall standoffs take M5 x 50, the electronics box M5 x 8 and the ring-foot grub screws are M4 x 10 (see the review notes below). The electronics box is the same, now centred under the rail.
- Ring centre 157 mm and ring top 216 mm above its rail bottom; the piece is about 0.45 m tall and 1.38 m wide.
- **Mirror retention:** see below.

## Mirror fitting: rimless, glued (default since 2026-11-18)
The first bezel version (a printed push-and-twist cup on each face) printed clunky and heavy and was removed, so the carrier has no bezels: the two acrylic discs are **glued flat to the carrier faces**, as on the 150 mm test unit, and the fitting is permanent. The carrier edge is 0.5 mm inside the disc (R 49.5 mm), so no printed edge shows from the front; the rim is 3 mm wide with one cross bar and the hinge hubs, with no pegs. Each unit drops the two bezels (10.8 g of the 63 g mirror group), and the ring clearance to the carrier grows from 0.5 mm to about 3.8 mm (the disc corner at R 50.2 against the 54 mm ring bore).

Glue: a ring of 1 mm acrylic foam tape (VHB type) on the rim and a strip on the bar per face, plus a few spots of clear 2-part epoxy for the permanent bond. Test on a scrap disc first (CA glue can craze acrylic) and leave the protective film on until the end. Press each disc flat while it sets. Keep the printed carrier flat, because a warped carrier shows through the mirror.

## Weight pad (2026-11-18)
The carrier has a solid 20 x 14 x 6 mm printed pad on one edge, hidden between the two discs, so the mirror hangs flat in the ring when the motor is at rest and the eight units sit parallel. It adds about 2 g to the mirror group (49 to 51 g), at about 41 mm from the axle: about 0.7 mN m of restoring torque, against an estimated 0.1 to 0.4 mN m of hinge friction. Print the carrier so the pad is the lowest edge at rest (the ring and carrier are fitted so the pad hangs below the axle). The effect on tumbling while spinning is untested: centrifugal force flattens a thin disc toward horizontal at speed, which a pad this small will not resist. Test with a blob of putty on the old carrier first if you want to size it; `PAD_W` and `PAD_D` at the top of the script set the size.

## Hinge bearing pockets (2026-11-18)
The turning inner race of each 684ZZ used to sit 0.1 mm from a flat printed floor, so it could rub on the PLA behind it. The pocket is now 0.4 mm deeper than the bearing, and there is a 6.4 mm counterbore 1.5 mm deep in the floor, wider than the inner race, so only the outer race touches the floor. Update after the first print: the pockets were too tight, which squeezes the thin 684ZZ outer race and adds drag. The pocket is now **9.14 mm across** (a slip fit) with a 0.4 mm lead-in chamfer at the mouth and a flat-topped teardrop roof so it prints round (the ring prints flat, +Y up). The floor relief is **7.2 mm** across with a 0.2 mm chamfer, so the ledge under the outer race is only about 1 mm wide and the inner race has about 0.6 mm of clearance even after shrinkage. Hold each bearing in place with a small drop of retaining compound or CA on the **outer race only**, never on the inner race or the shields.

## Rail screws (2026-11-18)
The 20-series rail has a 6.13 mm slot opening, 1.8 mm lips and a slot 6.1 mm deep; the M5 slide-in nut is 9 x 10 x 4.4 mm (6 mm neck 1.4 high, wings 1.4, chamfer 1.6). The screw-head counterbore in the spindle blocks and motor brackets is 2 mm deep (was 3), so an M5 x 35 stops about 1 mm short of the slot floor with the full nut thread engaged. A 3 mm counterbore left only 0.1 mm to the floor. The wall standoffs are 60 mm long, so their head seat is now 15 mm in from the wall and they take **M5 x 50** (tip about 1 mm short of the slot floor); an M5 x 35 there would end about 15 mm short of the nut.

## Pre-order review (2026-11-18)
A second review of everything you buy changed these lines. **Box screws M5 x 8** (an M5 x 12 bottoms on the rail's slot floor before the head seats). **Ring-foot grub screws M4 x 10** (the foot face is 10 mm from the rod, a 6 mm grub would not reach). **VHB tape 3 mm wide for the rim (about 5 m) plus 6 mm for the bars (about 2 m)**, and a line for clear 2-part epoxy. The lower 608ZZ now goes into the block from below and is pressed up against the cone ceiling, which leaves it 0.5 mm off the rail. Fit the standoffs to the rail before the rail goes on the wall (the screw heads are on the wall side), and fit the electronics box last, because it hides the middle standoff's lower wall screw; use pan-head M4 wall screws, as the flange holes have no countersink. Still to check with real parts: pulley length (28 mm), motor shaft length (24 mm; 22 mm is marginal, 20 mm too short), nothing else on the washer (now an 8 x 12 x 1 shim) and the motor lead connector.

## Wall standoffs (2026-11-18)
Each standoff carries about 1.3 kg (12.5 N) about 65 mm out from the wall, so about 0.8 N m of bending at the flange and about 22 N of pull on the upper wall screw. The arm stress is under 1 MPa, and the T-nut and the M5 x 50 clamp are far stronger than the load needs, so the weak points are the wall fixings and the sharp arm root. The standoff now has four 4 mm ribs from the flange onto the arm, above and below, either side of the screw line. Use proper fixings for the wall type (studs or masonry plugs, or cavity anchors in plasterboard) with washers under the M4 heads, and keep the piece away from radiators and direct sun, as PLA creeps when warm. Test by hanging about 5 kg on the rail for a few minutes before mounting the units.

## Cable route (2026-11-18)
Each motor cable runs in the rail's bottom slot under a black PVC slot cover strip (20 series, one 1000 mm length cut in two; the box hides the small gap in the middle). The cables leave the box through its top plate, straight up into the slot: a 13 x 7 mm slot hole in the top plate at each end, centred under the rail (about x = 442 and 540 in piece coordinates, clear of the two M5 mounts at x = 460 and 526). It is wide enough for the 4-pin plug to pass, so thread the motor-end plug up through it, lay the cable in the slot and press the cover over it. There are no clips on the rail, because a T-nut clip would sit in the same slot as the cable, and no grommets, because the motor leads no longer pass through the end walls (the BOM drops the grommet line). Deburr the hole edges. Power comes in through a 12 mm hole in the back wall of the box (the wall side, 28 mm in from the left end, at the same height as before), so the supply cable runs down the wall. The box is only 31 mm from the wall, so a long barrel plug will not fit; use a right-angle plug, or cut the plug off the supply lead and wire it to the terminal directly. The USB-C socket stays on the right wall. At each rail end the cable leaves the slot and runs about 55 mm to its motor; tie it to the bracket with a cable tie.

## Printing material (2026-11-18)
Matt PLA throughout is fine for this prototype: indoors, out of daylight, short runs. PETG or ASA would add nothing here, so there is no need to buy PETG. Two cautions with plain PLA, which softens at about 55 to 60 C and creeps under steady load:
- **Motor bracket in PLA-CF** (needs a hardened nozzle): it carries the 0.5 kg motor about 55 mm past the rail end plus the belt pull, and bolts to the warm motor face. The spindle blocks can be PLA-CF too, for the belt tension. Keep the TMC2209 at the low end of 1.2 to 1.5 A with idle current reduction on.
- **Plain PLA for the carriers and rings**: PLA-CF is more brittle, and the thin walls around the hinge bearing pockets need some give.
- Press heat-set inserts in gently and a little cooler than for PETG; thread-lock the M4 grub screws and recheck them, as PLA relaxes under clamp load over time.

## Checks and load
No clashes at any of three random tumble poses or two poses with random ring angles, none in the static pose, and every printed part is a single solid. Frame, motors and box about 2.0 kg plus 8 units about 1.8 kg: about 3.8 kg on the wall.

One unit turns about 138 g (mirror group 49 g, discs and carrier) with spin inertia about 1.1 to 1.6e-4 kg m2. With the rotor, four units on one motor are about 4.5 to 6.3e-4 kg m2, so **100 rad/s2 needs about 0.06 N m and 300 rad/s2 about 0.19 N m before friction**, against roughly 0.25 N m from a NEMA 17 at 24 V. That is a workable margin for each half; friction over four links is the thing to measure. The two halves are driven independently, so they can run different patterns.

## Open points
- 320 mm GT2 loops and 8 mm bore double-head pulleys are not in the baskets; check they exist before committing to the pitch.
- Print the new carrier (rim edge 0.5 mm inside the disc) and a ring with the relief behind the bearing pockets, and check that the tumble joint swings freely before the rest.
- 1.5 mm acrylic discs at 100 mm: dish and finish are unknown, so order one first. The spacer is now an 8 x 12 x 1 shim, so it should clear the 608ZZ shield and outer race; check it on the bench.
- The 4 mm ring walls and the 0.95 to 1.5 mm walls around the hinge bearing pockets are thin: press the bearings in gently (PLA is stiff but brittle, so ream the pockets rather than forcing them).
- Everything else (pulley length, motor shaft length, thread depth) is as in the 50 mm doc.

## Option: open low-drag hinge bearings (saved 2026-11-18, not adopted)
If the 684ZZ hinge bearings (medium friction from shields and grease) hold back the tumble, an open version is the upgrade route: [Ashley Bearings, budget 684 open miniature ball bearing 4 x 9 x 2.5 mm](https://www.ashleybearings.co.uk/budget-684-open-miniature-ball-bearing-4mm-x-9mm-x-25mm), £18.40 ex VAT for 20 plus about £7 postage.
- It is 2.5 mm wide, not 4 mm, so the CAD (`BRG = (4.0, 9.0, 4.0)` in the 100 mm variant) needs the pocket depth and axial positions changed. Radial fit (4 mm bore, 9 mm OD) is unchanged. **No CAD change made**: test the cheaper 684ZZ first (degrease, light oil) and decide after.
- Check whether they arrive oiled or dry; add a drop of light oil. The 608ZZ spindle bearings stay as they are.

## Carrier hubs 0.3 mm longer (2026-11-25)
The carrier hubs end **0.2 mm past the bearing mouth** (hub length 51.7 mm from the centre, was 51.4) instead of 0.1 mm short of it. Printed pockets sit the bearing 0.4 mm in when it is pressed fully home, which leaves about 0.2 mm each side (0.4 mm of axial play in all) instead of about 0.5 each side (1.0 mm). Another 0.1 mm of hub length would take it to 0.1 mm each side. **Press both bearings fully home onto the pocket floor before dropping the carrier in**: a bearing left flush with the mouth would jam against the hub, and an over-tight carrier adds bearing drag. The hub turns with the rod and the bearing's inner race, so it does not rub. If a unit is still too loose, a 4 mm shim washer (0.1 to 0.2 mm) on the rod between hub and bearing takes the rest up. Only the carrier needs reprinting.

## Spindle block bearing seats, same fixes as the ring (2026-11-25)

The two 608ZZ pockets in the spindle block are now 22.14 mm across (was 22.10), the same +0.14 mm over the bearing OD that gave the friction fit on the ring's 684ZZ pockets, and both pocket mouths have a 0.4 mm lead-in chamfer. Not changed: the relief behind the upper bearing (14 mm, already there), the 45 degree cone ceiling over the lower bearing (already clears the inner race), and the pocket depth (the ring's extra 0.4 mm protected its first printed layers; the block prints upright, and deepening the upper pocket would drop the pulley 0.4 mm). The 22.14 mm figure is untested on a block: print one and report whether it is too tight or too loose.

## Desk option: two printed feet instead of wall standoffs (2026-11-25)

`python3 cad/src/spinning_mirror_wall_row_50.py --mirror100 --desk` builds the six-mirror piece standing on a desk. Own outputs in `cad/out/spinning-mirror/spinning-mirror-wall-row-100-desk/` and `cad/media/previews/spinning-mirror-wall-row-100-desk/`, no BOM. Everything above the rail is identical to the wall build.

**Foot** (`print_desk_foot`, two off, identical): plate 54 mm along the rail (the same as the spindle blocks, so the feet line up with them from the front) x 100 mm front to back x 6 mm, rounded corners, with a 20 mm web up to the rail bottom and 45 degree gussets (prints plate down, no supports). Two M5 x 50 screws go up from the underside into T-nuts in the rail's **bottom** slot, 20 mm either side of the foot centre, heads in 10 mm counterbores 30 mm deep from the underside (reach them with a long hex key); a short key rib sits in the slot opening so the foot cannot twist. Four 15 mm recesses, 1 mm deep, near the front and back corners, take self-adhesive rubber pads. The feet sit under the outer units 1 and 6 (X = 0 and 700), a 700 mm span, well clear of the electronics box (X 237 to 462), which hangs 44 mm below the rail. The motor cables run in the rail's bottom slot from the box to the ends, past the feet's T-nuts and key rib: sort that out at assembly (cable routing is left to the builder). The rail is 75 mm above the desk, so the box is 31 mm clear. The screw seat is 45 mm below the rail, the same as the wall standoffs, so the same M5 x 50 fits.

**Stability** (computed from the model): 3.67 kg, centre of mass 105 mm above the desk on the rail centre line. It tips front to back at about 25 degrees and along the rail at about 66 degrees. Keep the rubber pads on; the tumbling mirrors will shake it.

**BOM difference from the wall build:** drop the 3 wall standoffs and the 2 x 3 + 2 M4 x 40 wall screws; M5 slide-in T-nuts +1 (4 for the feet instead of 3 for the standoffs); M5 x 50 screws 2 + 1 spare instead of 3 + 1; add 8 self-adhesive rubber pads (15 mm). The foot dimensions are untested on a printed part: print one and check the rib in the slot opening and the T-nut alignment.
